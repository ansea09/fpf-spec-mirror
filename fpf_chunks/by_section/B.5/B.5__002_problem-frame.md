### B.5:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when you have a question, surprising result or promising construction and need to choose the next reasoning contribution. A person, team or service can conduct the inquiry. Its commission may specify an answer to deliver, ask for help in forming the question, or leave the direction of inquiry open. The work can call for a construction, proof, explanation, observation or new question. Choose and connect those contributions by what the inquiry is to obtain.

When work continues without a question because its outcomes are treated as ordinary, B.5.PI provides an earlier entry. An ordinary handover or demonstration can yield a question or an early cue without a recognized anomaly. Use that contribution here when reasoning can take it further.

**First useful move.** Recover what the recipient needs to understand or make possible and what they have left you to choose. In your own inquiry you may be choosing the question itself. Use an existing answer if it settles the question. Recover any performance the commission also requires. Otherwise ask what a proposed next operation would establish even if it succeeded completely. Where it supplies a premise, identify what will connect that premise to the answer. Section 4.1.2 helps when the possible operations or distinctions are themselves unknown.

For example: “We need the highest component temperature, but our model reports a mean. Two states with the same mean can have different maxima. Next, determine which temperature differences this arrangement admits.” This redirects the inquiry before another model fit.

The practical gain is a useful answer or a better-founded next question. A correct answer to an inadequate formulation can otherwise consume the inquiry's effort.

Use a known calculation, proof, observation or qualified Method when it answers the question. An earlier observation can supply a value. A commission for an independent observation today also requires that observation under the agreed conditions. Detailed mathematical techniques, physical modeling and domain validation remain with their disciplines.
