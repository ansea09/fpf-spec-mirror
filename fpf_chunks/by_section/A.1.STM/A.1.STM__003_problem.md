### A.1.STM:1 - Problem

Division of labour lets different participants work on components, methods, descriptions, tools and provider organizations. Their local choices become coherent only if they can explain what common outcome those choices are intended to obtain. A component may meet its specification while the whole remains unusable; a provider may become efficient at producing something its intended users do not need. Local completion alone cannot settle the shared project purpose.

A **system-of-interest (SoI)** is the system whose intended use, and the benefit sought from it, give a cooperation of creators its common purpose. The participants connect their contributions to bringing about, changing, sustaining or retiring that system in light of its use and effects. A project account can carry this common SoI through one temporary body of work; each supporting project need not acquire a separate SoI. The same machine can be the SoI of its manufacturer's product undertaking and a means of production for a component manufacturer. Its identity need not change when that relation differs. Before a future system exists, describe the intended system and use without asserting an actual holder.

The **current subject of inquiry or local work** answers a different question: what must this participant understand, obtain or change now? It can be a component, test rig, creator organization, Method or episteme. For a system, **system-in-hand** or **our system** can identify that local subject once the participant's relation to it is clear. In organizational work the organization remains the creator being engineered, while its products supply the SoI and the use that justifies its capabilities. Treating the firm itself as the common purpose can make necessary product work appear to be a dispensable expense. Conversely, a product design that ignores the creators' means, rights and commitments may never become usable. The connected account must make both consequences visible in the decisions that allocate and provide work.

A project can also get every nearby statement locally right and lose the long dependency. A functioning argument can assume one construction while the creation plan can supply only another; capable contributors can require incompatible shared resources. Agreement on the SoI does not by itself make these answers jointly usable. One team improves a component, another produces a builder, and another measures operation, yet nobody can show how those results are expected to reach the SoI's use or which actual relations now connect them. A diagram arrow, the word *creates*, or a calendar sequence can hide that gap.

The opposite failure is to turn the reminder into a universal route. Then every result becomes a System, a plan becomes an existing holder, project Work becomes a network, and every arrow appears to be the same relation. A.1.STM develops the shared systemic reasoning while leaving each local truth with its subject pattern.
