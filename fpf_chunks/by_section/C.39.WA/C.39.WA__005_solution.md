### C.39.WA:4 - Solution

Keep the original subject available throughout construction, comparison and correction. Develop its organization, distinguish the relations used in consequential inferences, and follow the general grounds into choices about parts. Compare the account with the original question. Return a missing contribution to the operation that can supply it.

C.39 supplies general construction and explanation; C.41 supplies the design and use of an attention aid. This Method connects those contributions for accounts of a whole subject. The subject practice supplies its own knowledge, construction rules and evidence. C.39.QR supplies the broader return of partial work to an originating question. When that return exposes a missing whole-subject account, carry its question, scope, reader and available contributions into section 4.1 here; return the developed account or state what is still missing for the originating use. Use this Method directly when building the account is already the assigned work.

#### C.39.WA:4.1 - Recover the subject before accepting its decomposition

Read the initiating question and applicable clarifications. State in ordinary subject language what must become understandable or obtainable, for which reader or receiving action, and at which scope. Recover the source commitment when the task includes restoration of a body of knowledge. If the explanation must connect several holon kinds, ranges of composition and levels of model abstraction, retain that commission in the first restatement under C.39.QR:4.1. Construct the consequential connections through C.37.MM:4.1.1. A collection of sound local explanations can still leave the requested whole unexplained.

Derive an initial expectation from the question and the subject's organization. For an account of a discipline, ask which recurrent problems, objects, principles, Methods and relations the reader needs. For a proposed composite Method, ask what it obtains and which connections make that possible. For an existing system, recover its boundary, functioning, constituents and interactions relevant to the question. Different subjects require different grounds of unity.

This expectation is a proposal that substantive inquiry can correct. Keep its basis available so a change of scope or criterion can be explained. When depth is unclear, identify the consequential uncertainty and proceed with the content already required under the plausible readings. An unspecified depth in “explain mathematics” still permits recognizing that a single multiplication does not provide an account of the field.

The reviewer forms this expectation from the request and necessary subject grounds before relying on the author's readiness judgement. It can be a concise explanation of indispensable constructions and connections; it need not reproduce the author's final text.

**Questions:** What was asked about? Which question about that subject must be answered? What scope and limits were assigned? What would let the receiving reader continue?

#### C.39.WA:4.2 - Construct the organization the account must explain

When the explanation leaves open which parts and enclosing wholes to follow or which alternatives their models must distinguish, choose them for the reader’s task under C.39.SA:4. Recover or propose what connects the relevant contributions. Follow the required understanding or result backward to the objects, actions, relations and conditions on which it depends, then explain the proposed organization forward. C.39:4.2–4.3 supplies this constructive return when an operation or join is missing.

For a Method, explain inputs, actions, choices, intermediate results, common conditions, alternatives and returns. A branch is usable when the reader can distinguish its applicability; an iteration needs a reason to continue and a condition for stopping.

For a discipline, recover its field question and boundary, the knowledge contributions that explain or constrain reusable practice, and the methods by which practice tests and changes that knowledge. Explain how those contributions form the field's assembly and what their coupling makes possible. Distinguish a constituent from a supplier whose result the field uses. Retain competing explanations or methods with their conditions of use. A changed claim or method must reconnect to that assembly; C.20:4.1–4.5 supplies its construction and continuity criteria. Several independently usable Methods can belong in the account without being forced into one sequence. Explain which principles the families share, what guides a choice among them, and what their enactments can establish. Where one family uses results from another, explain that connection.

For an existing whole, establish its relevant constituents and organizing relations under its subject rule. For a proposed whole, explain how the arrangement could obtain the needed property and which premises remain to be established. A knowledge claim, a physical object and a reusable Method have different conditions of composition and identity.

**Which whole must the reader be able to explain?** When explaining a holon, identify it and the property or behavior at issue. Explain how its constituents and their organization bear on that property. A project can use a Method. Within a composite Method, B.1.5 asks whether two identified Methods stand in a constituent relation. The whole's construction must require the part's contribution or admit it as an alternative for a required contribution. A functional layer groups contributions by what they do. For a claim that one participant controls another’s action, identify how its decision can affect that action. If feedback is claimed, show how an observation enters a later decision. Use the relation on which the conclusion depends. Library.Reference:6.2 helps resolve familiar terms such as *level* and *layer*.

**How far does the explanation apply?** A record can explain what happened to an entire organization on one occasion. A principle common to several disciplines can govern just one decision within it. At a fixed level of model abstraction, ask whether the account of the constituent could remain true while the promised outcome of the whole fails. Keep the same extent of composition in view, such as an entire office in each setting. Ask which assertions hold when the discipline, local arrangement or individual occurrence changes. Extending an explanation to another setting needs grounds that its conditions still hold. If the task is to construct a more concrete model, use B.5.MR:4.2–4.3 to show that the new model has a feasible completion, that its permitted completions preserve the earlier assertions, and which previously admissible answer it rules out.

**Choose the range of composition and the level of model abstraction.** What must the recipient decide, for which occasions and by when? For a choice, ask which difference could lead the recipient to act differently. For an order to establish one value, start with that value. Trace it to the whole it concerns, the parts whose behaviour can change the answer, and any handover, shared resource or rule they depend on. Follow those connections far enough to settle the decision. At each one, choose a model that keeps distinguishable the cases that could change the answer. Explain which cases can be grouped, what makes the grouping adequate for this inquiry, and what change would require more detail. An existing model or an independently established bound can already suffice. This gives a reason to stop at different scales in different parts of the same whole.

Suppose the available records concern two stores and their transport service. One client wants the closing stock, another needs six crates delivered by noon today, and a third asks whether the service can fulfil that order on every shift. Choose what to model from the commission. For stock, consider which sites and goods belong in the balance. For today's shipment, trace the handover that can change the promise and retain its arrival time precisely enough to resolve the deadline. For all shifts, examine which other bookings and capacity limits the model must admit. A confirmed reservation may settle today's capacity without a model of the vehicle's internal construction.

When an explanation promises to work at every relevant level of composition, specify the eligible wholes. A stock balance can hold for each store and for a group of stores. Combining the accounts works when the stocks cover the group without overlap, the units and time window agree, and the two entries for an internal transfer cancel. Stock in transit must also be accounted for. These conditions explain why the balance survives regrouping. To explain whether a delivery can cross the same stores on time, follow the handovers and the capacity available for them. Separate balances leave that joint behavior open.

**What may we leave out before combining the parts?** If a known bound or direct observation already settles the decision about the whole, use it. To claim that a proposed summary calculation preserves a result, compare it with the result for the connected detailed arrangements. A derivation can cover all admitted arrangements without constructing them one by one. If two arrangements give the same summaries but call for different decisions, recover the missing connection, use an independent source that settles the question, or give the narrower answer the summaries support. A calibrated meter on a common supply, for instance, can give the present total without reconstructing its branch currents. An exact answer is recoverable when it is the same for every admitted arrangement with those summaries. Check that the proposed calculation actually gives that answer. A bound can already settle a limit even when the exact value is unknown.

Two stores can each report ten crates at closing time. For the consolidated stock count, twenty is correct under the balance conditions above. Now promise to deliver six of the first store's crates to the second by noon. Suppose stock and transfer capacity are the only limits. The same balances fit two configurations of the run that arrives by noon, one with room for eight crates and one with room for only four. Recover the available capacity before promising the delivery. For a network of stores, carry each intermediate result far enough to support the next handover. A conservation law can justify regrouping throughout its stated domain. A rule that works at each store alone leaves the handovers open. C.29.1:4.3–4.5 explains the comparison in both orders, and C.37.MM:4.2 identifies what arrangements the models admit together.

A useful general explanation supplies reasons for choices. It lets the reader say why these contributions belong together, what their connection makes possible, and what a changed condition would reopen. Where the available description says only “coordinate,” recover what must be made compatible, who or what supplies it, and how the resulting relation affects the whole.

**Questions:** What makes this the whole under discussion? Which relations explain the relevant property or result? Why this composition or organization? Which external conditions and alternatives matter?

#### C.39.WA:4.3 - Identify the relation used in each consequential transition

**A mantra for reasoning with models and methods.** *What must we establish or do, and about what? If the kind matters, which one, and what tells us that an object belongs to it? What relates which objects, and how? From which observations or premises, by which rule, can we draw what conclusion, for which cases? By what method can we produce what is needed or bring about or maintain the required condition? What must one operation supply to another? If a premise changes, what must we reconsider?*

For teaching, unfold these questions from the intended use. Supply the field's terms for the types and relations, and its operations for obtaining a result. Work through why the answers depend on one another. In practice, enter through what is available. A local record can lead back to the rule that gives it meaning. If that rule itself needs examination, make it the new subject and apply the questions to it. Later evidence about the same subject can reopen just the conclusions and actions that depended on the earlier evidence.

When teaching someone to design this comparison, give the commission and the ordinary materials from which the planner works. Let the participant choose the whole, the composition scales, the dependency to follow and which model distinctions to retain or merge. Ask what makes those choices sufficient and what would reopen them. Credit a different justified arrangement or a suitable existing model that the participant selected. If the task supplies the time resolution that decides the deadline, the participant can still demonstrate a choice of route and use of the given model. A correct calculation with all these choices supplied establishes application of the design.

Give each answer in the terms of the work. A field supplies criteria for recognizing instances of its kinds. To decide whether two records refer to the same instrument, use the instrument's identification conditions. Two instruments can satisfy the same kind criterion. A kind, a discipline and an individual instrument can each be what a claim is about. Name which one you mean. If a model loses a difference that the judgement depends on, amend the model or limit the judgement.

State the claim the evidence supports. In classification, apply the kind's criterion to the candidate. In a calculation, give the premises and the rule that yields the conclusion. To infer something about an operating system from a model, establish which correspondence and conditions permit the inference. Say which cases the claim covers. A.2.6 tests coverage when a proposed use requires membership in a declared claim scope. A Method has its own conditions of applicability. A contract can specify commissioned work, deliverables and acceptance conditions. Use the meaning of *scope* that the particular statement requires.

For an action, name the reusable way and what it must obtain or preserve under those conditions. Distinguish the conclusion reached, the report carrying it, its delivery, and any later change in the recipient's work. The report can be both the output of one operation and evidence used by another. State each relation with its own verb. A.6.P.WMR recovers the participants when calling something a “result” leaves its role unclear. If the goal is a change in the world, identify how to establish whether the change occurred.

For a move from one claim or subject to another, name the relation on which the inference relies, the kinds of its participants, what fills each relevant role here, and the direction of the inference. A multi-party relation can require more than two participants. If a boundary matters, identify what crosses it, between which places or organizations, and in which direction. Translating a record can leave the instrument it describes in the same laboratory. Use A.6.5 when declaring positions in a reusable relation signature. The following relations support different moves.

For a rule said to recur across composition scales, give grounds covering the eligible wholes and constituents. For a dependency between scales, identify the property that carries, its direction and the relation that licenses the inference. For a joint outcome, trace how the constituents are organized and which combinations can realize it. For example, consider the work that fulfils two orders in one service window. If a resource is available for four hours and three are already committed, at most one hour is free for additional work that needs exclusive time in the same window. A second order can still be fulfilled by the shared work itself when its result serves both recipients. The method used by that team determines what it can do in the hour. For a statement about Work already done, establish which performances occurred. For a claim assembled from reports, check which premises they support and whether they rely on the same observation.

A relation identifies how its participants are connected. A returned claim also needs an applicable inference rule. Separately, the resulting work must be compared with the original account question; C.39.QR:4.2–4.4 develops these two judgements.

These relations can coexist. A reusable calculation can be a constituent of one Method and supply a result to another. A discipline can contain other disciplines and use results from fields that are not its constituents. A representation can preserve some structure, omit some and introduce useful constructed objects. Explain the relevant correspondence before returning a conclusion to the represented subject.

**Check a claim of strict refinement.** When an account of a system, method or field treats one model as more concrete than another, keep the inquiry fixed. Show that at least one design, execution or other completion remains feasible, that each preserves the earlier assertions and that a previously admissible answer is excluded. B.5.MR gives the comparison for individual models and for models of their governing conventions. B.1 gives the grounds for a separate claim about constituents and wholes.

**Identify the subject and relation before moving.** Name what the claim concerns, the cases it covers and the use of the answer. “The laboratory's rule for releasing an instrument” identifies a starting point. If you need to reason from a particular record to the rule it cites, identify that record, the rule and the citation. A release record can direct you to the local rule under which a decision was made. Check the applicable edition and the issuing authority. To examine that rule in turn, compare it with the discipline's conditions for a valid verdict. To establish what actually happened, return to the identified instrument, configuration and observations. Carry forward only the conclusions for which that return supplies grounds.

A transdisciplinary distinction can guide reasoning within one discipline. Interdisciplinary work brings contributions from different disciplines into a common result. Ask which distinction guides the choice of types, or which contributions must be connected. Both questions can matter in one undertaking.

Locate the reasoning in terms the recipient uses. Identify the discipline that supplies the types, the local practice whose rule is being examined, or the occurrence to which a claim refers. A discipline can itself be the subject of a claim about its methods. To examine a field's type, ask what kind of distinction it makes and check it against the proposed higher-order criterion. In the other direction, propose a field type or relation, test its meaning against disciplinary practice and identify individual occurrences when the inquiry needs them. C.20 gives a test for the discipline as a whole. C.2.1 identifies the episteme stating a model or claim about the subject. A change of subject, a reclassification and a move to a constituent each license a different inference.

When the subject is a way of working, follow the **result and the operation** as well as the types. Start from the promised result, on what object and under which conditions. An agreed field procedure may supply the general operation with a domain-specific test, instruments or admissible evidence. Establish whether you have selected a setting within the same Method or changed the way and its applicability. One operation can use another's result through an external service. To claim a constituent Method, establish which reusable operations and connections make up the whole, how they jointly obtain its result and which replacements preserve it. Two comparable Methods can also be alternatives, each sufficient for the same use. A performed check on Tuesday establishes an occurrence under its own conditions. A.3.1:4.8 and B.1.5 give the grounds for these comparisons.

Grounding connects a case with an explained construction. Identify that construction and make its relevant objects, relations, operations and grounds available to the reader. Show which consequential moves in the case instantiate which operations, why the conditions select them, and what a change would reopen. In an account of a Method, distinguish the explained Method, its displayed performance and the subject worked on during that performance. For example, when explaining how to design an explanation, a calculation can be the material; selecting a contrast and revising the explanation are performances of the explained Method. E.8:4.2.1.1–1.3 and EXD.2 develop this correspondence for authoring explanations.

Choose meaningful variety from the account's declared subject and use. Differences in objects, relations, premises or available means can require different branches. Retain cases that make those differences understandable. A running case helps trace connected operations; add or change cases where it hides a consequential distinction, and return from each application to what it reveals about the general account. The choice of examples follows the work they must do, with no fixed number applying to every account.

When generalizing, state what elements and relations are being generalized, the wider class, retained premises and grounds for the extension. Varied cases can support construction, expose limits and contribute evidence under the relevant inference method. A general proof, a qualified empirical inference and a demonstrated performance have different warrants. Explain the general contribution and the role of the cases in obtaining or understanding it.

**Questions:** What was the subject before this transition, and what is it now? Which relation connects them, and what inference does it support? What explained construction is grounded by the cases? Which material differences do their variations reveal? What is generalized, over which range and on what grounds?

#### C.39.WA:4.4 - Return the general grounds to the parts

Use the account's general construction to choose, interpret, connect or revise its parts. Follow a consequential change of condition: which constituent action changes, which connection fails, which alternative becomes appropriate, and which contributions remain usable?

This is the constructive role of the whole explanation. For a family of Methods, a shared principle can explain why different branches use different means. For a composite Method, a whole requirement can constrain several concurrent actions. For a discipline, a changed knowledge claim can alter a practice's applicability and create a new inquiry. These are different returns with different grounds.

Then follow findings back into the common account. An unexpected case can reveal a missing premise, a new problem family or an incorrect connection. Revise the affected explanation and its uses. Keep the source's useful constructions while critically examining its claimed generality.

Distinguish the operations needed for that return. Synthesis connects contributions into an organized account or way. Generalization extends the range for which a claim or operation is warranted. Model abstraction leaves some determinations open while retaining the conclusion the receiving work needs; apply B.5.MR to compare the resulting model with the starting model. Selecting what an account displays can preserve all model assertions and leave the abstraction level unchanged. One piece of work can need several of these operations, with each contribution explained.

**Questions:** Which choices about parts follow from the common grounds? What changes when a consequential condition changes? What do varied cases reveal about the common explanation? Which general contribution still has to be invented by the reader?

#### C.39.WA:4.5 - Compare the actual account with the original question

Describe what the candidate explains, temporarily setting aside its title, field label and declaration of completeness. Compare that content with the expectation from section 4.1 across the assigned scope. Inspect the general construction and its relations as well as local claims. Recover what the cases concretize and which consequential variations of the declared subject they expose. Compare the knowledge and operations available in the general account with those displayed in its cases.

When the account solves a narrower problem and leaves the more general inference unwritten, use C.39.GA to find that step, restore its grounds and recheck the dependent conclusions. Keep the parts of the case that show how the inference works.

For an account of a principle, identify the relation the reader can recover and the decisions it supports when relevant conditions change. A single worked example can supply the whole explanation if it gives the argument for that range. If a completed case shows how someone used an already chosen rule, accept that demonstration. When the commission also asks how to construct the rule, return the account for the missing choices and their grounds. An explanation of why one particular event occurred can be complete once it establishes the requested connection for that event.

For each consequential conclusion, retain its subject, property, scope and grounds. Truth of a claim, membership in a field, coherence of a construction, coverage of an assigned account, usability by a reader and effect in work answer different questions. Select the conclusion the assignment needs and obtain its basis.

When source preservation is promised, F.0.2.RC tests whether the action, conditions, qualifications and returns can be reconstructed through the receiving explanation. Its source cut follows the promise: a commitment to a whole corpus requires that recovery basis. For a new synthesis, identify what the author constructed and how it is supported. A reader's own successful invention locates a useful contribution but does not make it content of the candidate.

Return one of the substantive outcomes the evidence supports: an adequate account for the named use; a statement of which construction is missing; a contradicted inference; an unavailable ground; or a changed, explicitly agreed question. Preserve a useful fragment with its qualified conclusion. Keep the original task open while a required contribution remains unavailable.

**Questions:** What does this text explain? Which required constructions and connections are supplied? Which are missing, unsupported or still unexamined? Did the subject or criterion change, and on what authority?

#### C.39.WA:4.6 - Arrange independent appraisal and complete the return

Select independence according to the intended conclusion and the observed difficulty. A reviewer who must judge the author's construction keeps authorship separate. A fresh reading context is useful when earlier narrowing or a disclosed expected answer could shape recognition. Supply the original question and necessary subject grounds before the candidate and earlier verdicts. A domain specialist can still be needed; freshness does not supply subject competence.

The reviewer compares the account with an independently grounded expectation and explains the missing relation or contribution in subject terms. The author develops that content or obtains its missing supplier. Reconsider the affected conclusion and dependent uses after repair. A coordinator accepting several contributions compares their coverage and joint conditions with the original result; C.41.SR supports that reconciliation.

Use the six question groups as an aid at the work occasions:

- **Before decomposition, read-do:** recover the subject and develop the expected organization in the explanation.
- **During construction, read-do:** follow the questions at a consequential join and construct or correct the missing content.
- **Before an adequacy claim, do-confirm:** use the performed work and its grounds to answer the questions about the complete relevant account.
- **At independent review:** recover the expectation first, then compare it with the full relevant candidate.
- **At acceptance:** connect the warranted contributions and unresolved conditions to the original requested result.

C.41.OF arranges access, timing and competent participation. C.41.UA separates obtaining an answer, deciding what to do and performing the resulting action. A negative or unresolved answer must have a useful continuation. Put the relevant outcome in the explanation, review or handoff already needed by the work.

Use C.41.QC when the question set or checking means is itself doubtful. A useful appraisal must distinguish a correct fragment from an account that supplies the required whole. A criterion that asks only whether the fragment is correct cannot support that decision.

After interruption, recover the original question, the relevant whole construction, the last supported contribution and the unresolved return. Reuse earlier grounds while their subjects, conditions and conclusions remain applicable. For an AI agent, keep the operative questions and subject material available in the working context. For people, provide the necessary time, access and help. Observe understanding, performance of the operation and the resulting benefit separately when deciding whether the arrangement works.
