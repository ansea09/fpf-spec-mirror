### C.41.QC:4 - Solution

A.10 governs evidence reliance; A.19.ULSAM governs a numerical aggregation. This qualification constructs the cases and expected distinctions needed to judge a criterion and its checking means. Separate answers need no aggregate, and an applicable earlier qualification needs no repetition merely because the questions are now carried in a checklist.

#### C.41.QC:4.1 - Recover the claim, basis and intended reliance

Name the criterion, the question it interprets and the subject of that question. Recover the description and the assumption connecting it to useful work. When the criterion needs construction, use [C.41](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c41---select-checklist-questions-for-a-work-result) §§4.1–4.2 to obtain a consequential work question and its basis. Use [C.39.RQ](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c39rq---construct-situated-method-requirements) when the missing criterion concerns a Method contribution.

Then name the checking means: a person applying a judgement, a comparison script, an observation tool or a model-based evaluator. What does it observe or consume? What answer does it produce? What will another person or system infer from that answer? Administrative claim-checking Methods supply the distinctions needed for that domain.

For a model judge, include the supplied evidence and instructions, the criteria grouped in each call, and any rule combining repeated verdicts when these can change the relied-on answer.

Separate two uncertainties. A criterion can faithfully express the description while the description poorly serves the work. A checking means can also fail to apply that otherwise useful criterion. Investigate the uncertainty that changes the intended use.

A cheap indirect signal can justify further examination without certifying the larger result when it passes. An obsolete edition label on the card box can prompt inspection of its contents; a current label alone does not establish that every card was replaced.

Name this intended inference and next move. Challenge both false reassurance and unnecessary alarms. If people can satisfy the visible signal while bypassing the substantive work, examine whether the signal remains informative for the intended use.

#### C.41.QC:4.2 - Construct a discriminating challenge

Choose a challenge whose answer could change the proposed reliance. Use cases with known or competently established differences relevant to the criterion. When qualification depends on detecting both a satisfactory result and a plausible failure, include both and vary the property of interest while keeping other differences small enough to interpret the verdict. One established false pass can already rule out a checker as a guarantee that every accepted item meets the criterion. For a subjective criterion, recover the judgement basis and a meaningful disagreement.

Predict which answers those differences support before inspecting the checker output. Then compare the outputs with adequate grounds for those expected distinctions. A false pass and a false failure can have different costs; inspect both where they affect the work.

Where a rubric may drive the verdict independently of the subject, also hold the subject facts fixed and change the question. Establish what answer the new predicate supports before asking the checker. A reversed condition can call for the opposite answer; if only wording changes and the question stays the same, the expected answer stays the same. This distinction tests responsiveness to meaning without requiring every wording change to reverse a result.

When a decision about the judging arrangement depends on whether an alternative would give adequate answers with less effort or interference, compare the alternatives on the grounded cases. A question about whether the existing arrangement can be relied on may already be settled by its observations or by a known failure.

Grouping criteria can save calls; separating them can reveal interference. Repetition and a rule for combining verdicts may reduce sampling instability, but repeated agreement cannot correct a consistent error or supply independent truth.

Select the arrangement from consequential errors and total effort; repeat only when that comparison can change reliance.

If the receiving use consumes a total or percentage, examine what that number means and how the questions affect it. Splitting one easy condition into ten items can increase its influence without improving the subject. Ten successful formatting checks may conceal one failed catalogue-preservation check. Challenge the implied priorities, duplicate contributions and treatment of missing answers. Keep consequential answers visible.

Where a numerical aggregate is needed, [FPF A.19.ULSAM](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#a19ulsam---unified-lawful-scale-aggregation-mechanism) governs the admitted measures and lawful aggregation; a convenient average does not supply its justification.

Start cheaply. One counterexample can defeat an overbroad claim. Use a direct executable comparison when the question and observable property admit it.

A selective rubric or pairwise judgement can help with an interpretive or relative question, but choosing the better of two results does not establish that either meets an absolute condition.

Qualify that judging arrangement on cases whose consequential answers have adequate grounds independent of the checker being evaluated. Compare false passes, false failures and obtaining those grounds as well as the cost per judgement.

Broader reliance may call for representative cases, independent judgement, executable observation or a field trial through [A.3.1.WT](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#a31wt---design-and-interpret-a-method-work-trial). Recover what must be independent. Two evaluators can make independent judgements about the same drawing against the same tolerance. Establish how each reached their answer under the required conditions. Their agreement supplies two assessments and still only one drawing. A requirement for a second source observation calls for that observation.

When work is repeatedly optimized against a score, compare earlier and later work results on consequential characteristics and suitable new cases. A rising score can reward surface changes while the intended result deteriorates. For the importer, a more persuasive success report leaves identifier loss unchanged. Inspect the proposed shortcut and obtain evidence beyond the optimized signal.

A stronger second model can help investigate disagreement, but its score needs grounds and does not become ground truth by rank.

#### C.41.QC:4.3 - State the bounded result and a return condition

State which decisions the qualification supports. A grounded check on one item can settle the disposition of that item. When relying on a checker across a class of items, establish why its observations and decision rule detect the differences that matter throughout that class, or bound the claim by the evidence available. Testing one checker on one kind of item and another checker on a different kind establishes what happened in those pairings. To use them interchangeably, examine the untried combinations that matter or establish why the change cannot affect the answer the recipient needs. If the intended decision permits a rate of error, evidence about that rate must cover the population and operating conditions to which it will be applied. Report detected defects and remaining uncertainty where they change what the recipient can accept or needs to inspect.

If the criterion misses a work question, return to C.41. If the form corrupts its meaning, return to C.41.TF. If access or capability is missing, return to C.41.OF or its supplier. A false pass, a false rejection or a failure to return a needed answer can justify withdrawing the checker from that use or limiting it to conditions for which adequate evidence already exists. Before relying on a repaired or replacement checker, establish that it gives the answer the criterion calls for under the affected conditions. Repeat the affected challenge if that evidence is still missing. Compare the gain with the qualification and operating cost through [C.11.MV](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c11mv---compare-the-worth-of-a-method-option).

Keep the qualification separate from subject answers. C.41.UA establishes what is true of the subject on its occasion.

Reopen qualification when a material criterion, model, tool, observation route or operating condition changes, or when a new counterexample defeats its scope. Changed evidence, instructions, criterion grouping or verdict combination can trigger this even with the same model and criterion.

Retain qualification for the unaffected observations and inferences. Stop when further investigation cannot improve the intended reliance at worthwhile cost, and preserve the limit rather than extending the claim.
