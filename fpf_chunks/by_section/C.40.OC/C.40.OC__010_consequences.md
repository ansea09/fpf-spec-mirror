### C.40.OC:9 - Consequences

Practitioners gain a characterization that is smaller than a full diagnosis yet strong enough to guide opportunity construction, source requests, finite comparison, or advice. Several scales and unknowns can coexist without being presented as one score. Protected conditions become visible before selection, and later premise changes can reopen only the claims that depended on them.

The cost is that a familiar word such as *maturity* may unfold into several claims with different owners and evidence windows. Some apparently precise numbers become conditional or unusable. Completing the account may require measurements or specialist results that the current inquiry cannot afford.

The characterization supports the inquiry. Obtaining a measurement, diagnosing a condition or changing the subject requires the applicable operation and evidence.
