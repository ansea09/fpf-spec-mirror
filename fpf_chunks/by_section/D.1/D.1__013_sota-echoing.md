### D.1:9 - SoTA-Echoing

For judging a person's participation in :2.2, **adapt** the developed consent reasoning in the [Belmont Report, “Respect for Persons” and “Informed Consent”](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/belmont-report/read-the-belmont-report/index.html) (1979), [UNESCO's bioethics declaration, Articles 5–7](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/universal-declaration-bioethics-and-human-rights) (2005), and the [Declaration of Helsinki, paragraphs 25–30](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/) (2024). Against treating expressed willingness or a sponsor's approval as sufficient, :2.2 examines the agreement's scope, information, understanding, voluntariness and applicable capacity or representation conditions. The participation case changes its conclusion when the claimed authority or an existing commitment changes.

These sources supply developed treatments in medicine and research; their requirements retain those domains, while D.1's broader argument remains conditional on its stated normative premises. Reopen that argument when a changed obligation, affected party, consent condition or domain rule changes the grounds of the judgement.
