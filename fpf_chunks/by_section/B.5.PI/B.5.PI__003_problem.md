### B.5.PI:2 - Problem

A worker receives enormous values from a familiar calculation and says, “It sometimes does that.” A team meets every deadline through repeated manual repair. An assistant answers a colleague's objection with a familiar recommendation, then treats the colleague's inability to name a better answer as closure. The work continues without a question about the unexplained part.

More searchable advice cannot help until something makes that advice worth seeking. “Ask for help when there is a problem” assumes the recognition that is missing. “Look for anomalies” leaves both the occasion for looking and the relevant comparison unspecified. A passed checklist establishes only the conditions it checked.

The difficulty is to let ordinary work generate a question without requiring a prior diagnosis, a permanent observer or an expert who knows the answer. The opposite error is to manufacture a defect whenever something differs from expectation, or to turn every small uncertainty into an expensive investigation.
