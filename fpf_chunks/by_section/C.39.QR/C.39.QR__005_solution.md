### C.39.QR:4 - Solution

Begin with the work the question calls for. Which practice, object or relation could have the property the recipient needs to understand or change? What material gives access to it? Choose an inquiry whose answer would advance that work. When a contribution comes back, establish what it supports, develop the missing connection and use it in the original work.

C.39 supplies construction of an obtaining way; the subject practice supplies its inference and composition rules. C.41 supplies the attention aid and its use. The operations below connect those contributions for this recurring return.

#### C.39.QR:4.1 - Recover the question independently of the proposed answer

Read the initiating words and applicable clarifications before choosing a way to answer. Distinguish the material the speaker supplies from what the question asks you to establish or choose and who needs the answer. If the subject is still open, work back from the conclusion or change sought. To explain why a practice repeatedly produces a result, identify how that result could arise and what would sustain its production across the occasions in question. Follow the relevant contributions and their organization. Distinguish the evidence that an outcome occurred from evidence for how it was produced and why it recurs. If several explanations still fit, choose grounds that would tell them apart where the difference matters to the commission. One occurrence can motivate a question about a class. A principle can be supplied for a decision about one occurrence. Where composition matters, recover the kinds of holon, their own constituent–whole relations and the range fixed or left to choose in the question. Where model abstraction matters, recover the inquiry, its interpretation and the distinctions the requested answer must preserve. State how an argument from a different subject, scale or model bears on that answer. A bound or a proof about one constituent can sometimes decide the whole question.

**Choose the question before developing the answer.** Read the speaker’s words in their conversational setting. A remark may invite acknowledgement, a request may call for an explanation, and an open invitation may leave the subject to the responder. Keep the particular question a proposed answer would settle beside what the speaker is trying to establish. Could that answer remain entirely correct in two admissible situations which require different conclusions, explanations or actions for the original purpose? If so, identify the missing distinction and its grounds before treating the answer as sufficient. Both answers can use the same vocabulary and concern the same topic. An accessible passage, calculation or reader trial can be a sensible starting point. Before letting its availability decide the next work, identify what a possible outcome would establish for the original question and which outcomes would leave that question open. An intermediate result may supply a premise, narrow a bound or change the support for an answer. It may reveal which source, experiment or collaborator makes the next inquiry feasible or less costly. Identify how the result could advance the commissioned work. When that connection is missing, establish it or choose an inquiry with a justified contribution. The issue can be the scope of a text, the composition of a holon, the abstraction of a model, or the kind of judgement being requested. If the grounds already settle the question, give the answer. If a different question would serve the recipient better, explain why and make the proposed change visible.

A question can contain a mistaken premise, a tentative explanation or a deliberate supposition. Establish which reading matters to the speaker. Evidence that the premise is false can itself answer the original inquiry. If the premise is uncertain, show how the answer depends on it or what would settle it. For a hypothetical question, reason under the stated assumption. A specialist entrusted with choosing how to help can examine a different cause or means while pursuing the same purpose. Explain the connection so the recipient can follow the correction. If it changes the goal or an independently promised result, bring the proposed change and its grounds to the party entitled to decide. Preserve any useful answer to the original difficulty.

**Keep the commission when choosing the next work or using a result.** A first restatement can already change the task. A later choice of plan, explanation or evidence can introduce the same loss. Compare what a colleague could deliver by following your words with the initiating commission and its clarifications. Identify who asked, who is to do the work and who will use it when these differ. Keep the receiving action, the recipient, the types of entities and actions in view, and any ranges the conclusion has to cover. A commission may concern several kinds of holons, different ranges of composition for each, and a range of model abstraction. It may ask about what holds at each level, a relation between levels, or something that depends on several together. Naming the end points leaves that last connection to be established. If the commission leaves someone to choose the ranges or connections, retain both the choice and the person or agent entrusted with it. The person entrusted with that choice can use C.39.SA:4 to make it and establish where to stop. An author teaching the choice supplies the method and preserves the reader’s opportunity to choose. The eventual recipient may simply need the resulting decision.

When a reply would settle a different question, restore the omitted work in the reply itself. An account of where the change occurred can finish a request for its location. For a request to explain a recurrence or change a practice, use that location as a possible source of evidence, develop the explanation or intervention and carry it back to the practice. When describing the change helps the recipient, name the particular loss. “The restatement changed the kind of action.” “The example shows what happens at one scale.” “We still owe an explanation of how decisions across those scales affect one another.” When a concrete example helps, introduce it as an example and state which part of the argument it illustrates. Return to the assigned types, ranges and receiving action before selecting the next edit, inference or handover. Set aside the choices fixed only for illustration and retain any general reasoning the example contains. Can a colleague recover what to do, for whom, over which ranges, and what would count as completion? If a whole family of decisions was reduced to one familiar decision, say so at that level and restore the family in the next instruction. Naming a different instance leaves the same omission. Use C.39.GA to restore the general argument in the reasoning or response when its example or a narrower class has taken its place. For a request confined to that one case, answering it can finish the job. C.37.MM:4.1.1 develops the joint work when several models are needed.

Distinguish the requirement from the author's proposed decomposition or solution. Derive an initial expectation from the requested use and competent subject knowledge. A proposal to explain a discipline needs its organizing problems, principles, Methods and relations at a suitable depth. A proposal to restore a service needs the relevant functioning and operating conditions. These expectations have different substantive bases.

Keep consequential uncertainty explicit. Obtain a clarification when materially different readings require an authorized choice; meanwhile continue the content required under the viable readings. A provisional expectation can change as the subject becomes better understood. Explain that correction and preserve the original commitment unless its authorized basis also changes.

A reviewer at risk of inheriting a narrowed question forms this expectation before relying on the candidate's outline or earlier verdict. The expectation states indispensable content or conditions, not a second complete solution. Obtain the initiating request and its applicable changes from a source that preserves them. The author can point to it and suggest questions. A public promise or title may already show that the offered work concerns a different subject, composition scale or level of model abstraction. Obtain the fuller terms where they affect the judgement.

**Questions:** What must become understood, decided or obtainable? For whom and under which conditions? What boundaries were assigned? Which expectations come from the subject, and which are only a proposed way?

##### C.39.QR:4.1.1 - Recover what “all” covers

Use this move when taking *all*, *every*, *always*, *never* or a similar expression literally would change a decision. Echo the word as a question to yourself: “Every single one?” Try one plausible exception. Who or what would count, under what conditions, and what is claimed, asked or required of each? A membership rule can identify the domain without a list of names. Establish separately the property or action claimed of its members.

Read the utterance in context. A literal “everyone” can cover exactly the people already identified in the conversation. An emphatic “everyone knows” can express impatience or a wish to move on. Preserve that purpose. To skip an explanation on the strength of an audience's knowledge, establish what the people who need to act already know. An explicit requirement holds on its agreed terms, including who owes an individual or a joint result and which exceptions it permits. A possible exception tests the reading. An established exception within the stated domain refutes a universal factual claim. An actual breach leaves the obligation in force under the governing terms.

For “at all levels”, recover what orders the levels in this question. Within any relevant kind of holon, part–whole relations can give scales of composition. A System and a Method each have their own. Control layers can be ordered by how one sets goals or constraints for decisions in another. Model abstraction compares what models admit for the same inquiry. Recover the relation the speaker uses and the ranges included, even if the field orders its levels in another way. Checking every immediate part of a whole can stay at one composition scale. Examining work at each of several scales can leave some parts unexamined. Recover whether the question asks what holds at each member, what carries between them or what they achieve together. “Each can use a method” can allow a different method for each. Promising one method usable by all adds a shared choice. Here the domain identifies the members. The logical scope of a quantifier identifies the part of the proposition it governs, including any choice made within it. Include combinations of ranges when the question or the organization of the work requires them.

Use the reading supported by the words, the agreed work and the situation. When several readings remain, give the answer they share if it suffices. If they lead to different decisions, state the difference or ask the speaker about the boundary that decides it. For a factual claim about every member, give an argument that covers the domain or adequate evidence covering each member. To determine what was promised to all, the authorized agreement can suffice. To judge feasibility, examine the promised work and its shared conditions. To report fulfilment, establish the outcomes the agreement calls for. Rhetorical emphasis gives no estimate of the proportion. For membership of a context slice in an FPF applicability scope, use A.2.6. The people, parts or models quantified over can vary within a single such slice.

#### C.39.QR:4.2 - Identify the relation used by the partial work

Name the original subject and the working construction in ordinary subject language. Establish how work on the latter can contribute to the former. Use the relation needed for the next judgement.

| Relation used | Grounds needed for the return |
| --- | --- |
| Case and kind; narrower and broader kind | Membership criteria or a directed generality relation, and separate grounds for any claim about the wider range. C.3 and C.3.1 supply the relevant distinctions. |
| Constituent and whole | The subject's composition, interactions and shared conditions. A.14, B.1.5, C.2.1 and C.20 govern different kinds of whole. |
| Method and performed work | The reusable operation, this occurrence, performer, inputs and conditions. A.3.1 and A.15.1 distinguish them. |
| Description, diagram or model and its subject | What is preserved, omitted or introduced, the correspondence being used, and the rule permitting the proposed inference. C.37 supports representation choice; C.29 supports mathematical model use. |
| Models at different levels of abstraction for the decision | What each model specifies, which assertion the decision needs, and what permits carrying the available result to that assertion. B.5.MR:4.2–4.3 supplies the comparison when refinement is claimed. |
| Sample and target population | The selection process, target and conditions of the statistical inference supplied by the relevant statistical practice. |
| Measure and valued result | What the measure tracks, under which conditions, and which value judgement remains. E.13 supports the return from a proxy to the value it is meant to serve. |
| Compressed and fuller account | The questions and meaning retained, consequential omissions and the return to the fuller basis. A.6.3.CSC supplies that scoped compression. |

Several relations can occur in the same work. A model of a component is a representation of that component; the component's relation to a system is composition. Keep both when the conclusion traverses both. An example can be an instance of a kind and illustrate an operation, with different consequences for each use.

Tracing a relation back identifies its other participant. Inferring a property of that participant requires an applicable rule. A correspondence can warrant selected inferences while leaving others open. For a many-to-one abstraction, recover the omitted distinctions when the returned question depends on them. The subject practice determines whether a deduction, empirical inference, construction or qualified analogy supplies the needed warrant.

**Questions:** What are the participants and relation? Which claim is being carried across it? What permits that inference under these conditions? What has been left out?

#### C.39.QR:4.3 - Establish what the performed work obtained

Examine the contribution and its grounds. State the claim or result it supports, its subject and conditions, and what was supplied by another source, performer or tool. A reader's additional construction can be useful; identify it as that reader's contribution when deciding what the received material supplied.

Carry a conclusion to the original subject only through the applicable relation and inference from section 4.2. If that inference is unsupported, repair it, obtain the missing ground or retain the narrower warranted conclusion. Preserve sound local work.

Ask what could have produced the same result. To accept a model for use, check its assertions against the intended use. To credit someone with developing it, compare the original assignment, models and help available with that person's or agent's actual choices. A correct submission may reproduce a completed form. It can pass a content check while its authorship and even who applied the rule remain open. A calculation trace can show application. A dated design decision can show who selected and justified a rule. Credit the choices the evidence identifies. The distinction matters when commissioning a design, assigning work or assessing what someone has learned.

A single feasible design can establish that the specified performance is possible in the model. One verified occurrence under the stated conditions can establish that it was achieved. If the claim says every permitted design has a property, a single feasible design lacking it refutes the claim. One verified late delivery likewise refutes “every delivery arrived on time.” For a claim that at least 95 percent arrived on time, count it against the specified population and period. For a requirement that every delivery arrive on time, record the breach and apply the terms in force then. They may preserve later deadlines, make a credit due or end an obligation on that breach. If they leave a remedy to a later decision, establish who may choose it and whether they did. To report that a credit was paid, establish the payment. A simulated failure can expose a defect in a design argument. Reporting a failure at an installation calls for evidence from that installation. In each case the next decision depends on which assertion the result bears on.

Then make the distinct comparison with the original task. Ask whether the result now available supplies the required answer, explanation or ability to continue. For example, a valid estimate of a system's load can support sizing while leaving a requested operating arrangement undeveloped. Conversely, an adequately supported narrow estimate may fully answer a request for that estimate.

Keep the two conclusions explicit when they differ: what is established about the subject, and what part of the assigned work is thereby completed. Truth, membership, fidelity, coherence, coverage, usability and beneficial effect require the grounds appropriate to each judgement.

**Questions:** What did this work establish, about what, and under which conditions? What is justified about the original subject? What required result remains unavailable?

#### C.39.QR:4.4 - Reconstruct the encompassing result

Compare the result now available with the expectation recovered in section 4.1. Examine the relations needed to obtain the encompassing result as well as the contributions themselves. Follow inputs and intermediate outputs, shared conditions, alternatives and returns at the level where they affect the requested use.

Use C.39:4.2–4.5 to construct a missing obtaining way or join. When the requested result is an explanation of an organized subject, C.39.WA develops its common grounds and organization, then brings those grounds back to choices about the parts. A whole can contain several independently usable Methods; a connected account need not force them into one sequence.

For promised source recovery, apply F.0.2.RC to the promised contribution and scope. For a system or service, use its engineering and operating methods to establish the relevant whole conditions. Where the missing contribution is capability, equipment, access or authority, obtain or revise that support through its responsible practice.

Synthesis connects contributions to obtain a result. Generalization widens the range for which a claim is warranted. Model abstraction constructs or selects a model that leaves some determinations open while retaining the conclusion the receiving work needs; apply B.5.MR to compare its assertions and admitted answers with the starting model. Changing what a description displays can leave the same model assertions recoverable and need not change its abstraction level. Choose the operation the gap requires, and explain its result. A wider field name cannot supply a missing connection.

Follow a changed encompassing condition to the contributions that relied on it. Follow a consequential finding in a contribution back to the encompassing account. Retain unaffected uses on the basis of their dependencies.

**Questions:** Which organization or connection does the requested result need? What governs the choice and change of the contributions? What missing content or support prevents the return?

#### C.39.QR:4.5 - Perform the useful continuation and settle its reach

Choose the continuation from the gap: develop content, correct the relation or inference, recover a source, obtain support, compare an alternative way, or seek an authorized change of the task. C.11.DUA compares further inquiry or change with continuing on the available basis, including the effort of learning, coordination, execution and displaced work.

Complete the selected contribution and return its result through sections 4.2–4.4. A changed original requirement can require a different comparison; make that change recoverable. A missing contribution keeps the corresponding requirement open even when another useful part is finished.

At a handover or acceptance decision, give the recipient the warranted result, the use it now supports and the consequential remainder. Reconcile several returns through their joins and shared conditions. C.41.SR supports this reconciliation. The recipient can accept a sufficient result, request the missing construction, retain a qualified partial result or decide a justified stop within its authority.

The work ends for this occasion when that decision is supported at the requested scope and the chosen continuation has been performed or transferred to its responsible recipient. A partial return can complete an assigned subtask while leaving the encompassing assignment open.

#### C.39.QR:4.6 - Put the return into ordinary work

Use the questions where a difference in their answers can change the work:

- Before a consequential decomposition or representation choice, recover the original question and the contribution expected from the smaller work.
- At a scope-changing inference or handover, establish what the partial result warrants and what the recipient still needs.
- Before a whole-result judgement, compare content and joint conditions with the original requirement.
- After an interruption or a changed condition, recover the affected connection and continue from it.

C.41.OF arranges the occasion, access and competent participation. C.41.UA connects obtaining an answer, deciding what to do and performing the action. Keep the subject material and these operations available at that occasion; a remembered slogan may be insufficient when several relations are being traversed.

Use another competent reader when independence or an unprompted initial judgement is needed. Give that reader the original question and necessary subject grounds before earlier verdicts or a suggested defect. Freshness can expose an inherited narrowing; domain competence supplies the grounds for the expectation. Select the arrangement by the judgement required, and reuse sound prior results within their scope.

For recovery after interruption, A.15.10 returns the purpose, operative method, established results, unfinished work and changed conditions to a useful continuation. For people and AI agents, examine separately recognition of this difficulty, performance of the relevant operations, and benefit in the receiving work. Reopen the aid when it repeatedly fails to expose a consequential loss or creates work without improving the answer.
