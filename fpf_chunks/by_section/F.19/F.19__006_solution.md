### F.19:4 - Solution

Use `OntologyFirstPlainRewrite` as one connected reading and repair over a natural sentence, row, paragraph, list, or small coherent section. Take the intended reader and use from the surrounding work; do not invent an adversarial reader or a persona form.



#### F.19:4.3 - One connected reading and repair


1. **Establish the reader's use and state the governing message.** From the passage, the request it answers and the material normally available to the reader, establish what this reader needs to recognize, understand, decide or do. A stated question or task, heading or surrounding explanation can supply the grounds. Keep a passage's local function distinct from the larger explanation the recipient requested. If different supported uses need different content, retain that uncertainty and obtain the missing purpose before judging sufficiency; continue semantic repairs that do not depend on it. A clear claim can have an unclear use. If the decision depends on composition scales or levels of model abstraction, apply F.19:4.3.1.1 during this reading. Then say what object, claim, action, event or distinction the span needs to convey. Mark process traces, status language, reference boilerplate, quality proof, defensive caveats, ornamental detail and other apparatus that may be displacing it. Truth or polish alone does not make the apparatus useful.
2. **Recover the predicate and its participants.** Identify the operation and every participant that changes it. This may be an actor, object, source, target, result, or another required operand. Apply the same question to verbal and relational nouns: recover *of what*, *for what*, *between what*, or another required participant. Leave an argument implicit only when one intended value is cheaply and uniquely recoverable from the local span.
3. **Check predicate compatibility.** Recover what the complete claim asserts under its negation, modality, or conditions, and test the subject and participants under the intended literal or metonymic reading. A positive assertion must assign its predicate to a compatible kind. A denial may correct an evidenced type mistake under the plausible-reader test below; without that ground, “evidence does not notice the error” introduces an idle alternative.

   Keep ordinary metonymy when the relation is established and the capable participant or work remains recoverable: a diagram may show, a framework may help, a reminder may cue, and a constraint may limit.
4. **Resolve referents and kinds.** Pronouns, demonstratives, omitted heads, and repeated labels must select one locally appropriate referent. Preserve the object kind, claim or relation kind, slot or relation position, use and publication boundary, and flow distinction whenever one changes the claim. A shared grammatical position does not make different FPF kinds interchangeable.
5. **Test contribution.** Read the complete span with and without each optional contrast, guard, modifier, example, coordinated member, or extra proposition. Remove it when the plausible intended reader can recognize, understand, decide, and act in the same way. Local truth and grammatical fit do not earn a phrase a place by themselves.
6. **Resolve coordination and lists on two axes.** Recover the proposition or action served by the series, its intended scope, and the relation among its members before choosing the form. Use one kind or predicate only when it fits every member. Distinguish a closed set, illustrative examples, alternatives, a sequence, several direct relations, and a failed ontology. A closed set needs its kind, membership rule, and closure. Connect illustrative examples to the proposition or kind they illustrate, with a non-exhaustive cue when a plausible reader could mistake them for a classification or a boundary of applicability.

   Usually state the proposition first. When an explanation starts from cases, make the transition to its general claim explicit and supply grounds for that claim's scope.

   Then decide whether the reader needs to distinguish or retain the members together. If so, preserve the series and the differences that matter to understanding or action. Otherwise state the governing claim or relation directly, retaining its scope and needed grounds. A question about one case may be answered by that case; a general explanation retains its general proposition and reasoning when illustrations are shortened.

   Test discourse load: keep each optional member for its distinct contribution; reduce repeated coordination and modifier chains that make the reader retain needless branches or postpone the governing message. Length is a reason to inspect the series. Decide from what the series contributes. If a series hides an FPF kind, relation, or structure that ordinary reading cannot recover, use the pattern that defines or tests it, or return the unresolved meaning as a blocker.
7. **Foreground, rewrite, and compare.** Put the governing event, claim, requested action, or decision before optional atmosphere, examples, caveats, and catalogues. A prerequisite may come first when it is needed for safe interpretation or action. Write the shortest ordinary technical sentence that preserves every live predicate and participant, established term, polarity, and action-changing detail. Such detail can include quantity or threshold, sequence or timing, criterion or tolerance, exception, and applicability.

   Compare before and after: any unsupported change of kind, relation, scope, use, currentness, or operational effect is a loss and blocks the rewrite unless another accepted decision authorizes it.

Keep ontology visible only where it carries the sentence. A term-source or type annotation is needed only when it changes how the reader identifies the object, kind, relation, slot, use, publication boundary, admissible use, or applicable rule. A record, card, table, schema, data structure, dashboard, or named form remains apparatus unless it carries one of those values.

If ordinary domain wording already preserves them, keep the ordinary sentence. "The aircraft flies" is better than a typed expansion unless the flight function, system kind, or slot relation is under repair.

##### F.19:4.3.1 - Keep what a passage claims and teaches through a change of wording

Read from the opening question to the decision, instruction or conclusion. What does the reader have to identify? Is the passage about one item, a kind and its criterion, a model or specification, a lot, or a family with a stated grouping rule? Is it reporting a dated action, explaining a repeatable way to act, or assigning the next job? The nouns can all be correctly typed while the answer slips from a rule for further cases into the story of one successful job.

If a second reading is plausible, try both on the work this reader has to do. Could a technician repair the wrong unit, a designer revise the product drawing when one unit needs repair, or a reviewer accept one successful check as grounds for every unit? Establish what the source actually supplies. State the missing identifier, criterion, condition or relation at the point where the readings diverge. If the evidence cannot decide between them, keep both readings open and obtain the particular fact the decision needs. Where the document already identifies the right unit or operation, use its ordinary name.

Follow the method as well as the object. If the text promises to teach a way to check an item, recover the operation, the conditions under which it applies and what would count as success. When repeatability is in question, try the wording on another item to which the rule should apply. The very same case may admit two ways to do the work. If the passage says which method was chosen or prescribed, identify the separate grounds for that choice. An account of what the technician did on Monday supplies one performance. Recover what permits repeating the check and which choices must be made again. Conversely, a report that one item passed calls for the record of its test. General instructions alone cannot establish that occurrence. Keep the field's own rule in view when moving from a transdisciplinary distinction to a discipline, or from that rule to a local procedure or job.

Write for the decision in front of the recipient. A furniture designer can write “Use drawing T, revision 4, for the seat joint.” The workshop order can read “Repair the loose joint on stool 17.” For an order to inspect the May lot, give the lot reference. If inspecting any available stool is sufficient, say so. One agent may need any of these forms. In “giraffes have long necks” the plural can express a characteristic of the kind. In “three giraffes came to the waterhole” it reports observed individuals. Singular wording can also speak of a kind or of one individual. Quantity, criterion and referent come from the statement in use. An explicit “all”, a particular set of items, or an exception belongs in the text when it changes what the reader may conclude or must do.

Reread the whole passage after the edit. Which object would two competent users pick, what claim could each support, and what operation would each perform? Can they still say why moving from the case to the wider rule, or applying that rule here, is warranted? If both readings already lead to the intended action for the same reason, the familiar wording is enough. Use C.3 to test a candidate against the kind criterion. Use A.2.6 to check whether a claim covers the conditions of use, and A.3.1 to identify a repeatable method. When a change of subject affects the conclusion or action, use C.39.WA:4.3 to find the relation and the grounds the subject practice supplies. When rewriting a model or specification, compare what the earlier and revised words allow under the same conditions. An example allowed by both can conceal a lost alternative or a newly permitted one. Use B.5.MR if the difference changes a decision.

First establish whether the passage lets the reader recover what each model asserts. Giving an ambiguous term its supported meaning may reveal the same assertions; it does not by itself make the model more concrete. Use B.5.MR when a claim about refinement or abstraction needs to compare the answers admitted by models whose assertions and interpretation can be recovered. The delivered wording is enough to begin this recovery; its production history need not be known.

If a needed assertion or relation still cannot be recovered from the passage and its available definitions, identify that gap and the inference or action it prevents. Compare each operation promised to the reader with what the passage enables. Use C.39.GA when the argument covers only a narrower class or when the promised way of explaining, choosing or constructing is missing even though every stated conclusion is sound. Keep a sufficient method at the reach it actually covers. For a missing subject premise, derivation or operation, obtain what the recipient needs from the corresponding practice. For the whole explanatory attempt, EXD.Reference:3 follows the conclusion to its premises and operations and then forward to the answer. Bring back the supported assertion or operation, its grounds and any unresolved condition; express it in the passage and repeat local recovery. A clearer sentence alone does not establish the missing premise.

###### F.19:4.3.1.1 - Check which composition scales and model abstraction levels a claim covers

Read the instruction as its recipient will receive it. Could someone follow it exactly and still do different work from the work promised? When wording such as “at all levels” affects the answer, recover what is ordered, which members the speaker included and what is asked about each or their connections. C.39.QR:4.1.1 gives the echo question and shows how to handle an emphatic use, a literal universal and unresolved readings. “What can vary?” lets a reader substitute another case. For a conclusion about composition, recover the relevant kind of holon, its whole and constituents, and the range of scales for each kind. Which ranges does the commission give, and which must the reader choose? When that selection is open, use C.39.SA. Look for alternatives the model merges although the recipient needs to distinguish them, and for choices the passage fixes although the recipient needs to keep them open. If the conclusion connects different kinds, recover the relation it needs, such as which dated work enacted the Method. If the decision uses several levels of model abstraction, ask what each model leaves open, which conclusion carries between them and in which direction. A more detailed account may leave the same alternatives open. Several models may supply different premises for one conclusion. Distinguish a rule that holds at each composition scale, a dependence across scales and a result of parts working together. A short question intended for separate use must carry the distinctions the reader needs there. A familiar term with an established meaning in the accompanying text can suffice.

**When the question changes, return to the original question (C.39.QR:4.1).** Read a restatement, delegated instruction or returned result as its recipient will encounter it. What could the recipient actually do or conclude by following it, and what work did the current commission call for? A passage can change the subject, who chooses, the population, a composition scale or what the model distinguishes while using the same terms throughout. Make the reason for the change clear to whoever will use the result for the original question. Establish what the new result warrants and whether it settles that question, supplies a premise or leaves it unresolved. Restore a displaced assignment or conclusion. A deliberately bounded inquiry can finish with its own answer. Give its performer the task and information appropriate to their part, preserving any choices or discoveries entrusted to them. The person or system drawing the wider conclusion is responsible for any grounds still needed. Use the revised commission if an authorized decision has changed it.

If the intended operation or its reach is unclear, restore it from the commission. If the meaning is clear but the argument offered for the whole range only works for a case, subclass or one permitted arrangement, **use C.39.GA, Restore a Missing General Argument**. Bring the repaired inference back to this passage and check the conclusions that depend on it. A general argument can already be complete inside one example. A report of progress can give a supported partial answer and explicitly leave the broader question open.

If the disputed step concerns a different relation, check that relation. For “layer”, C.30.STRAT:4.2 distinguishes a functional grouping from a scale of composition. To establish that a Method is part of another, use the construction of the encompassing Method under B.1.5.CM. For a claimed abstraction or refinement of models, compare what each admits for the same inquiry under B.5.MR. A transfer of one conclusion may need only a ground for that transfer. C.37.MM:4.1.1 helps when several kinds, scales or models must support one decision together. A claim that a permitted Method was actually performed needs evidence about the work under A.15.1. State what the available grounds establish when the stronger conclusion remains open.

##### F.19:4.3.2 - Restore a technical head when the meaning is still ambiguous

**Precision before a coarsened rendering.** When head kind, qualifier claim, or comparison basis remains unresolved in FPF-governed prose, use this working order:

Restore the head kind first. A narrowing qualifier such as `comparative`, `safe`, `interactive`, or `reliable` does **not** by itself restore that kind. Unpack the qualifier claim and then check whether the comparison or escalation basis is homogeneous. Only after that may a later Plain, didactic, or coarsened rendering relax the sentence, while keeping the more precise upstream interpretation recoverable.

Judge homogeneity against the comparison or condition rule actually used; recover distinct relations separately when that rule combines them. The basis may be a homogeneous claim-kind criterion, threshold, or named defining, constraining, or source-relation condition.

Treat `exact`, `direct`, `current`, `governed`, `subject`, `owner`, `defining`, and similar qualifiers as content only when they distinguish live alternatives. Remove them when no such contrast changes the truth, action, stop, or reliance.

A PatternID may remain an ordinary citation; expand it into a claim-bearing episteme, `ClaimGraph`, `U.MethodDescription`, `U.Method`, actor, assignment, `U.Work`, or another formal identity only when the current claim or a named later use depends on that distinction.

Keep ordinary practitioner action and instrumental pattern-use wording ordinary when it does not assert a particular dated Work occurrence. “Use `E.9` to record the decision” and “the framework maintainer compares the editions” need no invented Method, MethodDescription, performer, assignment, or Work identity.

Open the identity-bearing branch only when the sentence deliberately asserts a particular dated `U.Work` occurrence. Then point to its basis: A.13 first, independent A.15.1 Work admission second, and F.6 afterward only for precise assignment-bound attribution. Add a local system-role kind or a separate System-classification judgment only when that neighboring claim matters.

Treat a pattern episteme as a `U.MethodDescription` only after `A.3.2` establishes that it has an already admitted Method as its exact `EntityOfConcern` and makes at least one substantive claim about that Method as a way of doing. Otherwise cite the applicable pattern content as guidance and use `A.3.1` for the Method itself.



#### F.19:4.4 - Plausible-reader guards and cold-reader recovery


Use two reader tests for different decisions.

- The **plausible intended reader** has the knowledge and task presupposed by the text. Use this reader to decide whether a foil, guard, warning, or contrast deserves mention. Do not substitute an adversarial reader who can imagine any false inference, or the author who already knows the answer.
- The **cold intended reader** lacks the author's private context and unpublished notes. After the rewrite, ask whether this reader can recover the object, predicate, participants, relevant kind or ordinary status, relation, action-changing detail, and next useful action.

Recover the instruction from the publication, its stated prerequisites and the knowledge presupposed for its intended reader. An application-test scenario may supply task-specific inputs, such as workload or available resources. Distinguish those inputs from explanations of the instruction supplied only for the test.

If only the test scenario explains the action denoted by B2, successful application demonstrates use of that supplied explanation; the publication's wording question remains unanswered. Name or reference the action in the publication, then check recovery without the test-only explanation.

Retain a negative alternative, denied consequence, warning, or non-use statement only when three conditions hold. The particular reading being rejected has an independent local ground. A plausible intended reader could adopt that reading here, including through an evidenced type mistake. The distinction changes truth, understanding, selection, safety, stop, reliance, or action.

An earlier or source claim, an observed recurring mistake, a serious competing position, a visible representation feature, or an applicable safety risk can supply the ground. The guard itself cannot.

Use the smallest clear correction for a grounded guard. When actor allocation is the useful content, state it positively: “On receiving new evidence, the reader decides whether to reopen checking or revision.” When currentness is the useful content, state the direct use: “This guide conveys the seminar of 1 February 2026; check current rules against the current FPF edition.” Keep material negation, documented anti-patterns, fair disputes, and safety stops when their polarity or boundary is itself the claim.



#### F.19:4.5 - Basis and coverage of a recovery judgement


State whether the judgement rests on an expert walkthrough, an actual reading, or a formal-model estimate, using the distinctions in `C.2.8:4.5`.

An expert can judge recoverability from the public text for the intended reader. An actual response establishes what that reader recovered under those reading conditions. A formal-model estimate retains its stated observer, selected structure and model conditions under `C.2.8:4.6`.

Use expert reading for a bounded wording judgement when it resolves the question from the permitted public inputs and no actual response is required. Obtain actual public-only recovery when:

- the intended conclusion claims that a reader actually recovered the instruction;
- plausible reconstructions still differ in an action-changing participant, operation, result or condition, and the reader's response can change the repair or use decision;
- it remains materially uncertain whether the publication supplies the needed instruction or the evaluator supplies it from private knowledge or test-only explanation; or
- demonstrated misses undermine the sufficiency of the earlier expert reading for the affected instruction family.

A visible wording defect can be repaired directly. Select any reading still needed for the repaired text and intended conclusion.

Give a cold reader the publication, its stated prerequisites and task inputs, withholding private rationale and suggested answers. Preserve the initial recovery before supplying further explanation; qualify subsequent source returns or help under `C.2.8:4.3`.

Limit a recovery conclusion to the natural text units and relations examined under its actual evidence basis. A whole-publication conclusion needs coverage of the whole publication, including relations needed to read its parts together. Successful application probes cover their tested uses; they leave unexamined text outside that result.

A whole expert reading combined with local actual readings retains that mixed basis.

Reuse a result for the same or explicitly unaffected text, recovery question, reader preparation, access, assistance and budget when those conditions still apply. A local revalidation by a reader familiar with the earlier wording retains that qualification.

State a material limit in the receiving judgement or result already needed by the work. When a required reading is unavailable, identify the missing basis and leave only the dependent conclusion open.

Correct recovery of what the text asserts and the subject-matter warrant or practical adequacy of that assertion remain separate questions under `C.2.8`.



#### F.19:4.6 - Result and local revalidation


Keep wording that already serves the reader. Where a change is needed, return the repaired text or identify the meaning that still needs to be established. Do not require a separate record for each facet of the reading, whether a form, card, table, progress row, or written answer.

After changing words or syntax, reread the changed sentence with the passages needed to determine its referents, predicate, participants, contrast, modality, support, action, and result. Revisit a directly affected instruction wherever it appears.

The earlier semantic verdict does not transfer to new wording. Reuse a judgement about an unchanged passage when the repair leaves its meaning and use unaffected. A local edit does not require a new reading of the whole document.

When a named high-risk or disputed decision needs inspectable evidence, show the before text, repaired text, live values that had to survive, and any unresolved blocker. Use the receiving decision's existing comparison or review result. The optional fields below can structure that result when its receiver needs them; ordinary corrections need no separate form.

Before finishing this reading, can the recipient recover the intended work and carry it out at the reach the passage promises? For unresolved wording, consult E.10, E.10.ARCH, E.10.ROLE, A.6.F, F.18 or the definition needed. If following the text or using its result would answer a different question, use C.39.QR:4.1 within this reading to recover the intended work and check what the available result supplies. When the recipient needs to choose composition scales or levels of model abstraction, use C.39.SA through F.19:4.3.1.1. That section also identifies the relevant subject method for a disputed relation or functional layer. If a narrower way of arguing or explaining has displaced the promised one, return the missing step through C.39.GA. Follow the repaired step only into the conclusions it affects. Finish when ordinary reading and the available grounds suffice for the assigned use.

#### F.19:4.2 - Pattern-prose specialization

When the repaired prose is an FPF pattern, apply the same method with one purpose test:

> Does this sentence help the pattern's intended user recognize and perform the pattern, or does it record development, review, projection, landing, quality, or source-management evidence about this version?

If it records evidence about the pattern version, keep that evidence outside the pattern unless the pattern's own primary `EntityOfConcern` is that evaluation or projection object. The evidence can cause edits to the pattern; it is not automatically pattern content.

Pattern prose keeps:

- the pattern's own primary `EntityOfConcern`;
- the first useful move;
- the practical delta and cost of missing it;
- a local boundary that passes F.19:4's full independent-ground, plausible-reader, contribution, and smallest-clear-correction test; and
- short references to related patterns after the pattern's own content is visible.

Public architectural reasons stay with the explanation when they help the intended user understand, select, combine, or adapt the Methods and profiles. Apply `E.8:4.2.3` to distinguish those reasons from the current version's development history.

Pattern prose moves out:


- rationale for the current draft's placement or promotion as a development decision;
- correspondence about producing or reviewing the draft rather than using the pattern;
- quality, projection, monolith-parity, landing, and source-management evidence; and
- repeated boundary doctrine already carried by another pattern.
