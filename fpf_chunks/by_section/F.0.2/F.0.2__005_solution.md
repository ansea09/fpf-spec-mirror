### F.0.2:4 - Solution

#### F.0.2:4.1 - Bounded synthesis method

1. **Start from the difference the recipient needs explained or decided.** Identify what could have the property, make the choice or undergo the change in question. What must a satisfactory account let this recipient distinguish, and what choices are theirs? If an earlier synthesis already answers that question under the applicable conditions, use it directly. A commission to construct a new account still calls for that work. A comparison of terms can finish with their meanings. A question about how several parties accomplish a job calls for an account of their contributions to that job. When the question leaves the choice of wholes, parts or model distinctions to you, use :4.1.1 to choose the reach of the comparison.
2. **Locate material for that question.** Reuse known passages that address it. If a source, rival explanation or connecting premise is missing, use F.1 to locate material you can inspect. A source may matter because it connects two other contributions whose meanings are clear.
3. **Recover and select the claims that matter.** Identify what each source asserts, about which subject and under which interpretation and conditions. Keep the edition and its role in the argument recoverable. Use F.0.1 for a disputed local meaning, and F.9 if the argument needs a specific relation between local senses. Select sufficient grounds for the receiving question, including a consequential rival or limit. Reopen source selection if the comparison reveals a gap.
4. **Construct and test the account.** For each source, ask which part of the recipient’s question it can answer in its own terms. What grounds allow a conclusion from one account to enter the other? When each source answers a different part, follow its contribution to the point where the recipient needs them together. Try leaving their separate claims true while changing how the described things are connected. If the answer changes, construct a candidate connection. Identify the participants each source distinguishes and propose what would have to hold between them in the same case. Derive its consequence for the recipient. Check the connection against the source conditions and any case that could distinguish it from a competing account. Keep a conditional explanation when the grounds support only that much. If the accounts rival one another, use a difference in their consequences to decide between them where it matters (B.5.TC). Keep the conclusion they share when that already answers the question.
5. **Give the resulting account.** A provisional synthesis states what the source contributions warrant together and why. A contrast explains a consequential difference that survives the comparison. When a source gap prevents either conclusion, identify the unresolved inquiry and carry forward whatever the available material already establishes.
6. **Return the result to its intended use.** Show which conclusion the recipient can now draw or qualify and on what grounds. If the commission asks who did the synthesis, recover the material each contributor received. Which distinction was already in a source? Who added one or connected claims across sources, and how did that change the inference? A specialist may supply a premise and another reader a connection, or a team may construct the connection together. Attribute only what the work supports at the requested scale. When the work informs construction of a method, identify the operation or condition it changes. For a framework contribution, name the content or placement decision it informs. Keep the source edition recoverable and give the result a stable locator when it will be cited or replayed. C.2.1 governs an explicitly identified claim episteme.
7. **Decide what to do with the result.** Use the options for its branch in §4.3. When the receiving use requires a record, state the decision and cite the exact result and source edition. A result awaiting the decision remains a proposal; silence or citation alone is not acceptance.
8. **Keep the source use revisable.** Identify the source claims and editions relied on, why each matters to this result, what was deliberately left local or rejected, and which change reopens the comparison.

Stop when the result answers the receiving question to the extent the sources allow. If a material premise is missing, state what remains open and what would settle it. Enter clustering, Concept-Set development, naming, UTS or broad source harvesting when the next use needs that further result.

##### F.0.2:4.1.1 - Choose the reach of the comparison

Start at the subject the question names. If it names only an effect, ask what could produce or change that effect. If the recipient asks about a whole, consider whether the same constituent contributions could be organized differently with a different outcome. If the question starts with a part, consider whether putting it to another use changes what follows for the enclosing whole. Trace the composition of each kind that matters. For example, the acts that made up one job can differ from the operations prescribed by its method. Evidence that an act took place can connect those two accounts when it also establishes which operation was performed. At each scale, compare cases a proposed model treats alike. If they require different answers or different explanations that the recipient needs, restore the distinction and test the inference again. To simplify the model, establish why the inference survives the variation you propose to leave open. C.39.SA:4 develops this choice. B.5.MR:4.2 tests a claim that one model strictly refines another for the same inquiry and interpretation.

A rule applying separately to each part, a conclusion that carries from one scale or model to another, and a conclusion about what the parts obtain together require different grounds. For a transfer, identify the condition preserved in the required direction. For a joint inference, work out what follows when the premises concern the same arrangement (C.37.MM:4.1.1). One feasible arrangement can establish possibility. To explain what happened, establish that the relevant conditions held together and give the grounds connecting them to that outcome. For a guarantee, show why the required result follows across the permitted arrangements. A direct argument about the whole can settle the question without reconstructing every part. If a source itself gives the argument for the entire range, use it directly.

#### F.0.2:4.2 - Source-basis branches

| Source situation | How to use the source | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An admitted maintained synthesis already integrates a field | Recover every occurrence that can change the receiving question. Follow its cited sources where a load-bearing distinction or limit depends on them, and compare current alternatives that could change the answer. | The synthesis is a valuable conceptual map, not independent confirmation of its own integrated claims. |
| Several independent sources answer the question | Give each source an explicit role: proposed contribution, rival explanation, counterexample, boundary, evidence limit, or current alternative. | A reading list or source count does not establish coverage or relevance. |
| An identified `G.2` pack already exists | Reuse its identified source claims, editions, alignment records, and provenance when they answer the bounded question. | Pack conformance, a fusion record, or a coverage measure does not establish the receiving-framework claim. |
| An earlier DPF supplies a lesson | Classify the lesson as direct FPF reuse, a bounded FPF improvement proposal, or a domain/local claim retained in the receiving DPF. | Precedent does not create ecosystem law or prescribe package shape. |
| A derived lookup proposes FPF or DPF contributions | Return each result to the authoritative pattern body and edition. Report a partial result when a source is unavailable, stale, or incompletely indexed, and widen the search when a known contribution is absent. | A lookup result aids discovery; non-return is not evidence that a contribution is absent. |

#### F.0.2:4.3 - Three result branches

A result identified as a claim-bearing episteme retains its C.2.1 identity `<claim content, EntityOfConcern, effective ReferenceScheme>`. Keep the result and source edition recoverable for the receiving use. A stable local locator can supply a needed address for later citation or replay; it does not identify the claim by itself.

| Result | What the claim concerns | What it says | Required receiving disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Provisional synthesis claim** | The proposed account of the subject, with the interpretation used in the comparison and the correspondences to the sources. | A positive cross-source claim with its scope, source roles, retained differences and limits. It is available for the recipient to examine. | The recipient can use the claim with its limits, revise it as a separately identifiable claim, decline it for this use or reopen the comparison. |
| **Contrast claim** | The candidate unification or proposed shared contribution being tested, interpreted through the receiving scheme and the source-local comparison rules. | Why the inspected comparison does not warrant that unification for the named use. It preserves the source-local claims and any narrower positive claim that remains supported. | The receiving decision keeps the source claims distinct for the named use, adopts a narrower or revised claim, does not use the contrast, or reopens the comparison. |
| **Unresolved-inquiry claim** | The bounded inquiry and its available sources, interpreted under the stated scheme and comparison rules. | Identify which necessary premise the available material leaves open, why it matters and which conclusions remain warranted. | The recipient can act on a warranted partial answer, obtain a source or other evidence that could settle the question, or end this use with the limit recorded. Where an authorized choice has yet to be made, the recipient may commission that choice. Any later substantive answer has its own grounds. |

A finding about what the sources establish can itself answer the receiving question. If two authoritative rules leave the choice of the next participant to the commissioning party, the comparison can establish that those rules assign nobody. Making the appointment then requires an authorized decision. If a needed premise remains genuinely unresolved, explain its effect and whether a further observation, source or decision could settle it. A later user who needs to cite this result needs an identifiable account of it.

#### F.0.2:4.4 - Bounded method and broad SoTA-pack method

Use `F.0.2` when a bounded comparison of source claims, their interpretations and their consequences can answer the recipient’s inquiry with a synthesis, a contrast or a statement of what remains unresolved. Use `G.2` when a `CG-Frame` needs a broad, refreshable harvesting surface with a CorpusLedger, Claim Sheets, palette, inventories, alignment records, examples, and declared G.3-G.5 handoffs.

An author may use identified claims and provenance from a `G.2` pack as inputs here. The author must compare retained differences, limits, receiving use, and reopen conditions before writing an `F.0.2` result. The bounded comparison does not require the rest of the pack when the question does not use it.

#### F.0.2:4.5 - Boundary to wording-use precision restoration

Semantic synthesis and wording restoration both pass through ontology, but they answer different questions. Semantic synthesis forms or revises a cross-source claim. Wording restoration starts from an already current subject claim and repairs a consequential wording use so that the intended entity, relation, claim kind, and admissible action can again be recovered.

A recurring wording failure may supply evidence that a subject distinction is missing or unstable. The system maintaining the affected FPF or DPF edition then uses that evidence in a named content decision. The findings can support a proposed revision. The content decision governs changes to the claim maintained in that edition. A DPF keeps its domain wording entries beside the domain patterns that use them; `E.10.ARCH` supplies the shared restoration method.

A DPF may need a reliable current domain ontology so practitioners can recognize situations, distinguish Methods and results, and use its solution moves. An author can use F.0.2 to synthesize or revise that ontology as a proposed contribution. Ontology alone is not a DPF: use `E.4` and `E.4.DPF` to connect it to recurring problems, constructive Methods, a usable first cut, evidence practice, access, and maintenance.

#### F.0.2:4.6 - Semantic and methodological results

A conceptual synthesis states what the source accounts warrant about an identified subject, which kinds and relations support the claim, and where a disagreement remains. This can be the result a recipient asked for, including a result about actions or methods.

When the requested result is a new or revised way of working, call the whole undertaking **methodological synthesis**. Use established concepts where they suffice. If the source accounts disagree about what an operation acts on, what it supplies or what counts as success, compare those accounts. An agreement can supply a shared premise. If different readings are warranted under different conditions, provide for each in the method. If a premise remains open, obtain the evidence, use a way that works under either reading, or withhold the conclusion that depends on it. Trying the method can in turn reopen a conceptual conclusion. When the comparison is also needed as a reusable explanation in its own right, name both deliverables and what supports each. One recipient can need both in a single commission.

Carry each result to the scope at which its recipient will use it. A transdisciplinary way of reasoning can guide the work of a single discipline. Identify the conclusion at stake, examine how the field's types and relations warrant it and revisit dependent conclusions when a premise changes. In interdisciplinary work, identify what each field supplies and who needs each result. If a field uses a result from another, establish the correspondence it relies on. A recipient may use independent findings together. Identify the subject, conditions or constraint they must share for that recipient’s conclusion to follow. When two contributions appear to conflict, establish whether they concern the same subject under the same conditions. If the accounts are consistent but the products cannot work together, identifying the incompatibility may answer the inquiry. When the purpose requires joint operation, consider a change to the arrangement or the products and test whether it resolves the incompatibility. If the claims still conflict, identify what further evidence or revised account could resolve the conflict. Carry forward the conclusions whose grounds hold. A discipline supplies the particular means by which those grounds can be obtained or tested. A field method can be complete at this scope before a project selects staff, tools or a schedule. In a project, choose an applicable way and recover the local evidence needed for the next action. Reuse a known way when it suffices. Where several pattern Solutions supply the Method, show how their operations contribute to the outcome. Establish which results feed another operation and which must fit together at the point of use. If a needed result is unavailable, give the affected user an alternative or a way to revisit the choice. This is how a language of patterns expresses a way to act across cases. Use the mantra in C.39.WA:4.3 when an inference changes what the claim concerns, extends it to other cases, or changes which way applies. Name the relation and the grounds for that particular move. C.39.RO:4.2–4.5 develops the grounds for generalizing an operation.

Use C.39 to construct, connect, explain or change ways of obtaining the needed results. E.4.CM applies that general methodological work to a framework author's public account of one composite Method or several independent Methods. Those constructions can use the semantic result returned here; trying a proposed way can expose a missing distinction or reopen source comparison. A sufficient existing Method can be used directly.
