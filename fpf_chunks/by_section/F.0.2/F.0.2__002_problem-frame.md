### F.0.2:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when what someone can conclude or do depends on how different source accounts fit together or disagree. The sources can address the same question or supply different premises needed to answer it. The recipient may need the comparison itself, a way to act that depends on it, or a contribution to a framework pattern.

In this pattern, **conceptual synthesis** and **semantic synthesis** name the same work. It means comparing and developing claims through the concepts, relations and boundaries used by their sources. It can concern a theory of action or any other subject. When undertaken to construct or revise a method, this conceptual work is part of methodological synthesis. Its conclusions can also answer an inquiry in their own right.

Begin with the question the recipient actually asked. What would two different answers to it lead them to believe or do? Use that difference to choose the source claims worth comparing. A familiar word can lead you to a passage about only one part of the subject. Recover its local meaning through F.0.1 when it matters, and use F.1 when the choice of sources needs justification.

**What goes wrong if missed.** An author either leaves a crosswalk or literature narrative where a positive contribution is needed, or merges similar words before checking whether the sources describe the same entity, relation, explanation, and use. A large research package may also be demanded for a question that one bounded comparison can settle.

**What changes in practice.** The recipient can use claims from different sources to explain a relation, distinguish accounts that lead to different conclusions, or identify what still prevents an answer. When developing a method, use the result to build or change the operation that needs it.

**Not this pattern when.** Cite one source directly when one source claim closes the question. Use `F.1` when only source selection is current; `F.0.1` when only local meaning is unclear; `F.9` when one relation between already recovered cross-context senses is current; and the subject pattern when it already supplies the needed action and result. Use `G.2` instead when the work needs a broad, refreshable `SoTA Synthesis Pack@CG-Frame` and its downstream Part G handoffs.
