### E.15:9 - Consequences

**Benefits.** Small repairs stay small. Material changes show which uses depend on the changed content. Historical editions remain usable. Independent predecessor probes make large rewrites safer. Alternative search remains available where it can improve a decision, and whole-pattern plain-language checking keeps ontological precision usable.

**Costs and limits.** Determining which uses depend on a change and which functions the predecessor served requires semantic judgment. A repository search cannot fully answer either question. A Δ-2 or Δ-3 repair can be expensive when many consumers depend on the changed premise. This pattern reduces redundant checking but cannot make a broad semantic change local by declaration.

Reopen the method when the four Delta-Classes no longer distinguish action-relevant change, when dependency-focused verification demonstrably misses affected uses, or when a lower-effort method provides equal or better preservation and decision quality.
