### C.39.GA:4 - Solution

**A mantra for restoring a general argument.** *What must the reader explain, construct or decide, and which choices are theirs? Which kinds of holon, over which composition scales for each kind? What connects these kinds? What holds at each scale, follows from part to whole or from whole to part, or depends on the parts working together? Which levels of abstraction of the relevant models does that work need, and which alternatives remain open at each? Which conclusion carries between models, in which direction, or needs several models together? Which promised choice or operation can the reader still make only because it was settled in the material? Where does the argument rely on a condition fixed only for a narrower use? Restore the way to make that choice or the grounds for the inference. Which later conclusions follow, under which conditions, and what is still owed?* Use the questions about composition and models when the work calls for those relations.

1. **Recover the promise.** From the commission, state the result the recipient must obtain or explain, which choices they must make and what can vary. For a claim across scales, identify the whole for each relevant kind of holon. Does that result need a rule at each scale, a link between scales or a result of several working together? For a range of model abstraction, ask what the model may leave open.
2. **Locate the lost step.** Work back from that conclusion to the premises it needs. Read forward from what the passage, its accessible sources and the audience’s preparation supply. Where does an inference first depend on a choice already made for this case or subclass? Could two rules give the same answer here and different answers under another permitted condition? Retain an argument made inside the example when it supplies grounds the recipient needs. There is a gap if the recipient needs an inference and has no adequate grounds for it in the account, its accessible sources or the agreed preparation. Also start from each operation promised to the recipient. Which inputs and ordinary help would be available to them before that choice was settled? A sound argument for that answer can leave the promised way of constructing an explanation wholly absent. Two different ways can both be adequate when the recipient is free to choose either.
3. **Repair the inference where it is used.** For a transfer, name the property to be established and the direction of the inference. Trace a permitted situation through the relation to the receiving situation. Show why the property is preserved, or identify a permitted exception. When several premises are needed, establish the relation between their subjects. A record may concern the very job being assessed, an earlier test may cover the new operating condition, or two actions may compete for the same resource. When a shared constraint restricts combinations, establish which combinations it permits. One feasible combination can establish possibility. A promise that must hold throughout a range needs grounds covering every admissible combination in that range. For an explanation of how to choose or build something, identify which needed choice the material already settles. Starting from the information the recipient actually has, develop a way to find alternatives and distinguish the ones that matter to the promised use. If the promise is to explain an organization, trace how its constituent contributions fit together and what their arrangement makes possible. Two arrangements that justify the same immediate action may require different explanations. A known working construction can yield a reusable operation under C.39.RO. Develop a missing construction in its subject practice. Keep a worked case when it supplies a needed inference or helps the recipient recognize when to use it. Show which fact or choice in the case does that work. If the recipient could complete it by using a distinction the author supplied but was meant to teach them to choose, restore the choice at the point of use. Replace or remove an episode that contributes nothing to the commissioned work. A sufficient direct argument can lead straight to application. If the grounds run out, give the supported answer and identify what is still owed.
4. **Recheck the reach.** Follow the repaired step through the conclusions that depend on it. A derivation for an arbitrary member, a sufficient bound or an exhaustive division into cases can cover a class. An empirical claim needs observations and grounds for extending them to the population and conditions claimed. When the missing work was a choice or construction, follow the repaired procedure from what the recipient has before that choice. Check whether the recipient can now make the entrusted choice, obtain the result and give the promised grounds with the help available there. Another admissible construction can be equally adequate. A changed case can expose an error. One sound argument can already cover the range, even when it gives different answers for different inputs.
